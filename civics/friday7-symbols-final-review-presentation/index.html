PART 1 · SYMBOLS

Two places to know

TEST QUESTION 119

What is the capital of the United States?

Washington, D.C.

D.C. = District of Columbia. Not Washington State! Iowa's capital is Des Moines (Q62).

TEST QUESTION 120

Where is the Statue of Liberty?

  • New York (Harbor)
  • Liberty Island

Also accepted: New Jersey, near New York City, on the Hudson (River).

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

Q119: the photo is the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., where Congress meets. Watch for the trap: some kids pick "Washington State" or "New York City." Also, don't mix up the U.S. capital (Washington, D.C.) with Iowa's capital (Des Moines, Q62). Q120: Wait, that's real? The Statue of Liberty was a gift from France, dedicated in 1886. Liberty Island sits in New York Harbor but is so close to New Jersey that USCIS accepts "New Jersey" too. Teach one answer: New York Harbor. (2 minutes)

The flag tells a story

TEST QUESTION 121 ★

Why does the flag have 13 stripes?

  • (Because there were) 13 original colonies
  • (Because the stripes) represent the original colonies

TEST QUESTION 122

Why does the flag have 50 stars?

  • (Because there is) one star for each state
  • (Because) each star represents a state
  • (Because there are) 50 states

MEMORY TRICK

STRIPES = START (the 13 colonies). STARS = STATES (all 50).

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

Q121 is a starred question. Point to the flag: 13 stripes, 7 red and 6 white, for the 13 original colonies. 50 stars, one for each state. Hawaii became the 50th state in 1959, and the 50-star flag started on July 4, 1960. Where kids go wrong: they say the stripes stand for "13 states." The answer is the 13 original COLONIES. Words in parentheses are extra. "13 original colonies" is enough. (2 minutes)

A song and a motto

TEST QUESTION 123 · THE NATIONAL ANTHEM

The Star-Spangled Banner

Francis Scott Key wrote it in 1814. He saw this flag still flying over Fort McHenry in Baltimore after a British attack.

TEST QUESTION 124 · “E PLURIBUS UNUM”

“Out of many, one”

Also accepted: “We all become one.” It's Latin. It's on the Great Seal (above) and on U.S. coins.

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

Q123: the photo is the real flag Key saw in 1814. It's in the Smithsonian today. Wait, that's real? It has 15 stars and 15 stripes, because back then the country added a stripe for each new state. Congress went back to 13 stripes in 1818. The song became the official national anthem in 1931. Ask: where do you hear it? (Ballgames, the Olympics.) Q124: E Pluribus Unum = out of many, one. Many states and many people become one nation. If anyone has a coin, have them find the words. Trap: students confuse the motto with the Pledge or "In God We Trust." The question asks about the FIRST motto. (2 minutes)

PART 1 · HOLIDAYS

Independence Day

TEST QUESTION 125

What is Independence Day?

  • A holiday to celebrate U.S. independence (from Britain)
  • The country's birthday

It's July 4. The Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776 (Q79).

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

Q125: two answers, and either one counts. "The country's birthday" is the easiest. Link back to Friday 4: July 4, 1776 is when the Declaration of Independence was adopted (Q79). Trap: kids sometimes say it celebrates the Constitution. No: the Constitution is 1787. Ask: if July 4 is the country's birthday, how old did the U.S. turn on July 4, 2026? (250 years old.) (1 minute)

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

Q126 is starred. Students need THREE, and all three must come from this list of 11. Easiest set: New Year's Day, Independence Day, Thanksgiving. Most kids get a day off for these. Trap: on multiple choice, a wrong answer will slip in something like Halloween, Valentine's Day, or Easter. Those are real celebrations, but they are not federal holidays. Juneteenth (June 19) became a federal holiday in 2021. It marks the day in 1865 when enslaved people in Galveston, Texas, learned they were free. (1–2 minutes)

Don't mix these two up

TEST QUESTION 127 · LAST MONDAY IN MAY

Memorial Day

A holiday to honor soldiers who died in military service.

TEST QUESTION 128 · NOVEMBER 11

Veterans Day

A holiday to honor people in the (U.S.) military: everyone who served.

MEMORY TRICK

MEMORIAL = in memory of those who died. VETERANS = all who served.

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

This is the most-missed pair in this section. Memorial Day (last Monday in May) honors those who DIED serving. Veterans Day (November 11) honors everyone who served. The left photo: volunteers place a small flag at every grave at Arlington National Cemetery before Memorial Day. The right photo is local: the Veterans Memorial Building on Mays Island in Cedar Rapids. Wait, that's real? Its giant stained-glass window was designed by Iowa artist Grant Wood (the American Gothic painter). It shows six soldiers from six American wars. It's free to visit on weekdays. Ask: does anyone have a family member who is a veteran? (Keep it voluntary.) (2–3 minutes)

Symbols and holidays honor ideas

Civic virtue = putting your community and country ahead of just yourself. These remind us of it.

Statue of Liberty

→ liberty (freedom)

“Out of many, one”

→ unity: many people, one nation

The flag

→ 50 states, one country

Independence Day

→ self-government

Memorial Day

→ sacrifice for others

Veterans Day

→ service to the country

MLK Jr. Day

→ equality and civil rights

Juneteenth

→ freedom: the end of slavery

Exit ticket preview: you'll pick one and explain the idea it honors.

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

This is the "why" behind the last 10 questions (standard Gov.16, civic virtue). Civic virtue means caring about the common good, not just yourself. Gold = holidays that honor people who served others. Turn-and-talk (1 minute): pick one box. Why would a country make a holiday or symbol for that idea? Listen for: it reminds people what the country is supposed to stand for, and it thanks people who served. Keep it balanced: people can disagree about how well the country lives up to these ideas. That's fair to discuss. (2 minutes)

Numbers that trip people up

27

amendments in all (Q7 ★). NOT 10. The first 10 are the Bill of Rights.

100

U.S. senators (Q21). 2 per state (Q27) × 50 states.

435

voting members of the House (Q24). More people = more reps (Q35).

9

seats on the Supreme Court (Q53). 5 votes decide a case (Q54).

13 · 50

stripes = original colonies. Stars = states (Q121 ★, Q122).

1776 · 1787

Declaration (Q79) comes before the Constitution (Q82).

18

years old to vote: one of the voting amendments (Q63).

22nd

Amendment: the President can serve only two terms (Q37).

Final Review · Iowa Civics Test, Friday 7

These are the numbers students mix up most. Quick drill: say a number, students say what it goes with. 27 vs. 10 is the number one trap on the whole test. 100 senators is easy if you remember 2 per state times 50 states. 435 is the House: it changes by state population, so big states like California have many more than Iowa (Iowa has 4). Iowa has 4 U.S. representatives. (2–3 minutes)

Memory trick: 6, 2, 4, life. Senators get the longest elected term (6). House members face voters every 2 years, so they have to stay close to what people think right now (Q26). PART 3 · TEST DAY

How the real Iowa test works

THE RULES

  • Multiple choice
  • 60% correct to pass
  • No time limit
  • Retake it as many times as you need
  • A teacher watches (proctors) it

SMART MOVES

  • Read EVERY choice before you pick.
  • Cross out the silly ones first.
  • “Name one” = any ONE from the list counts.
  • Words in ( ) are extra: “(Thomas) Jefferson.”
  • “Now” = whoever has the job that day.
  • Stuck? Skip it. Come back later.

Source: Iowa Department of Education, civics test FAQ (Senate File 369)

Facts from the Iowa Department of Education FAQ: it's multiple choice, built from the USCIS civics questions; you need 60% correct to pass; it's not timed; you can retake it as many times as you need; it must be proctored (a teacher watches). Students with an IEP, 504 plan, or English learner status can get changes (like smaller sections). Model "cross out the silly ones" with a sample: How many amendments? A) 10 B) 27 C) 50 D) 435. Cross out 50 (states) and 435 (House). Then pick between 10 and 27: 10 is just the Bill of Rights, so 27. (3 minutes)

GAME TIME · ABOUT 22 MINUTES · NO TECH

Civics Showdown

1. Write it. Teacher reads a question. Your team writes ONE answer in 10 seconds.

2. Show it. On “Show me!” every board goes up at once. Right = 1 point. ★ question = 2 points.

3. Steal it. Hot Seat round: if a team misses, the first other team with the right answer up steals the points.

FINAL WAGER: bet 0–5 of your points on one last ★ question. Right = win them. Wrong = lose them.

Teams of 2–3 · one whiteboard or sheet of paper per team · Seven Fridays → Hot Seat → Final Wager

Setup (1 min): teams of 2 or 3, one whiteboard or paper and a marker per team, a team name on the board. Keep score on the board. ROUND 1, SEVEN FRIDAYS (about 8 min): every team answers every question. 1 point each, 2 points for a ★ question. Max 21. Answer key: F1a ★ How many amendments does the Constitution have? = 27. F1b ★ What is the economic system of the U.S.? = Capitalism or free market economy. F2a How many U.S. senators are there? = 100. F2b ★ Who vetoes bills? = The President. F3a ★ What do we show loyalty to when we say the Pledge of Allegiance? = The United States or the flag. F3b Name one way Americans can serve their country. = Vote, pay taxes, obey the law, serve in the military, run for office, or work for local, state, or federal government. F4a ★ Who wrote the Declaration of Independence? = (Thomas) Jefferson. F4b What founding document was written in 1787? = The (U.S.) Constitution. F5a What territory did the U.S. buy from France in 1803? = Louisiana (Territory). F5b Name the U.S. war between the North and the South. = The Civil War. F6a ★ What major event happened on September 11, 2001? = Terrorists attacked the United States. F6b Who was president during the Great Depression and World War II? = (Franklin) Roosevelt. F7a ★ Why does the flag have 13 stripes? = 13 original colonies. F7b What is Veterans Day? = A holiday to honor people in the (U.S.) military. Rounds 2 and 3 are on the next slide's notes. Rule: spelling doesn't count if the answer is clear. Teacher's call is final.
