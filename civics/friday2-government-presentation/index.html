TEST QUESTION 28

Why does each state have two senators?

  • Equal representation (for small states)
  • The Great Compromise (Connecticut Compromise)

Compromise = a deal where both sides give a little. 1787 deal: House by population, Senate equal.

How the Government Works · Iowa Civics Test, Section 2

Q26: public opinion = what most people think right now. House members face voters every 2 years, so they have to keep listening. Q35: more people = more representatives. Iowa has 4. California has 52. Q28: at the Constitutional Convention, big states wanted seats based on population and small states wanted every state equal. The deal (the Great Compromise, also called the Connecticut Compromise) gave them both: the House by population, the Senate equal. Iowa connection: Iowa is a smaller state, so the Senate gives Iowa the same 2 votes as California. Discussion: is that fair? Give one reason yes and one reason no. (4 minutes)

BIG IDEA 3 · THE PRESIDENT

Q49: Why is the Electoral College important?

TEST QUESTION 49 · ACCEPTED ANSWERS

  • It decides who is elected president.
  • It provides a compromise between the popular election of the president and congressional selection.

Popular election = everyone votes. Congressional selection = Congress picks. The Electoral College is a deal in the middle.

IOWA’S MATH

4 representatives + 2 senators = 6 electoral votes. 538 total. 270 to win.

WATCH: TED-Ed: Does your vote count? The Electoral College explained (5 min)

Video (about 5 minutes, link at the bottom): TED-Ed, 'Does your vote count? The Electoral College explained.' Map: each state's number of electoral votes. Every state gets one elector for each member of Congress: House seats + 2 senators. Iowa = 4 + 2 = 6. 538 total (435 + 100 + 3 for D.C.). A candidate needs 270, which is more than half. Easiest answer to remember: 'It decides who is elected president.' Where kids get it wrong: they think the Electoral College is a school. It's not a school. It's a group of people (electors) who cast the official votes for president. Keep it neutral if the 'is it fair?' debate starts; one minute each side, then move on. (7 minutes with video)

How the branches check each other

Q45

Must approve (confirm) the judges and Cabinet members he picks

This slide pulls the three branches together (Gov.6, 7, 8). It's what the written exit question asks about. BIG IDEA 5 · FEDERAL VS. STATE

Q60: The 10th Amendment

TEST QUESTION 60

What is the purpose of the 10th Amendment?

  • (It states that the) powers not given to the federal government belong to the states or to the people.

Short version: if the Constitution doesn’t give a power to the federal government, it belongs to the states or the people.

CASE FILE · IOWA

The Constitution never mentions schools. So who runs your school? Iowa and your local school board. That’s the 10th Amendment at work.

Splitting power between the nation and the states is called federalism.

How the Government Works · Iowa Civics Test, Section 2

Federalism (Gov.9) = power is split between the national government and the state governments. The Constitution lists what the federal government can do. Everything else is left to the states or the people. That's why Iowa and Illinois can have different rules for things like schools and driver's licenses. Keep it simple: one answer, and students just need to recognize it. A good wrong-answer trap is 'freedom of speech' (that's the 1st Amendment). (2 minutes)

It matters so one branch doesn't get too powerful, and so laws have to follow the Constitution. Give full credit for 'checks and balances' or 'judicial review' with a reason. Students can do it on paper or tap 'Answer the exit ticket online' on this slide. Anyone who misses #1 or #2 should replay Zone 2 or the Final Boss in the game, or review the study sheet. (5 minutes)
